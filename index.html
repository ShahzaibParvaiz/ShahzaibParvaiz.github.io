Sultan Mehmed II (Fatih / Pen Name: "Avnî") Divan Murabba • "Ey Vay Gönül"
15th-Century Ottoman Classical Divan
Stanza I — Gönül Ey Vây Gönül Original & English
"Sevdün ol dilberi söz eslemedün vây gönül
Eyledün kendüzüni âleme rüsvây gönül
Saña cevr eylemede kılmaz o pervây gönül
Cevre sabr eyleyimezsin nideyin hây gönül
Gönül ey vây gönül, vây gönül ey vây gönül!"
English Translation:
"You fell for that enchanting beauty and heeded no counsel, alas, O heart!
You have disgraced yourself before the entire world, O heart!
In inflicting torment upon you, the beloved shows no restraint, O heart!
Yet you cannot endure the torment with patience—what am I to do with you, O heart?
O heart, alas my heart, woe to this heart, alas O heart!"
Closing Stanza (Mahlas of "Avnî") Pen Name Verse
"Bilmedüm derd-i dilün ölmek imiş dermânı
Öleyin derd ile tek görmeyeyin hicrânı
Mihnet ü derd ü gama olmağıçün erzânî
Avniyâ sencileyin mihnet ü gam-keş kânı
Gönül ey vây gönül, vây gönül ey vây gönül!"
English Translation:
"I knew not that the only cure for the heart's affliction was death itself;
Let me perish in this sorrow rather than witness the agony of separation.
Deemed worthy of every trial, anguish, and grief—
O Avnî, where else exists a soul so steeped in sorrow as yours?
O heart, alas my heart, woe to this heart, alas O heart!"
Why "Ey Vay Gönül" Resonates: Sultan Mehmed II conquered Constantinople at 21, yet in his private Divan poetry he wrote under the humble pen name "Avnî" (The Helper / Seeker of Aid)—reminding us that no matter what external systems or empires one builds, the hardest and noblest mastery is over one's own heart (Gönül).
